How the Numbers Are Calculated

Per-placement base incentive (unchanged from current scheme): Each recruiter's base pay uses marginal slab brackets — the first set of placements at the Slab 1 rate, the next set at the Slab 2 rate, and so on. Placements with a hiring/sourcing fee (HSR) and without it are counted on separate ladders. Enterprise Consumer/BFSI slabs break at 35 and 70 placements; Airtel at 30 and 60; Corporate at 25 and 50. HSR rates are ₹150/200/300; non-HSR rates are ₹130/175/250. Recruiters below their vertical's target earn nothing. Because the base incentive is identical to the current scheme, it cancels out — every rupee of cost change comes from the retention and sourcing bonuses.

Retention bonus (new cost, accrues monthly): For each eligible recruiter, every placement earns an additional amount per month the worker stays (capped at 12 months). The per-month rate depends on which slab tier that placement falls in. Team Leads and Managers receive the same per-month rates, applied to their attributed placements (capped at 100). The assumed improvement is that workers stay 1 additional month on average, generating extra markup revenue for the firm but also incurring retention bonus cost across all months of the worker's tenure — including the months they would have stayed without the bonus.

Sourcing bonus (new cost, paid once at placement): 5% of all placements (not just target-eligible) are assumed to be cross-recruiter sourced, since a recruiter can share a candidate regardless of whether they meet their own placement target. Each cross-sourced placement carries a one-time sourcing bonus set as a fraction of the placer's average base rate. Of these, half are assumed to be genuinely new placements (creating new revenue and new placement costs), and half are substitution (same total placements, just the sourcing bonus cost).

Revenue: For each recruiter, monthly revenue = average monthly markup per placement × number of placements. The assumed 1 additional month of retention increases the number of months each worker stays, adding one extra month of markup revenue per placement. Net new placements from cross-sourcing also generate markup revenue and sourcing fees.

Recruiter Incentive Simulator

168 recruiters · 7 TLs · 15 Managers · 4,152 PRs · March 2026 actuals · Per-person simulation

Controls
Attrition improvement — the retention bonus (y × months) incentivises better screening. This slider models how much longer placements last as a result. Applies to new scheme only; status quo stays at baseline tenure.
Cross-recruiter placements — the sourcing bonus (z) pays recruiters for sharing candidates. This slider sets what % of placements are cross-sourced. Status quo has zero cross-sourcing.
% net new — of cross-sourced placements, how many are truly incremental vs just swaps? At 0% it's pure cost; at 100%, every cross-sourced placement brings new revenue.
Threshold — minimum PRs to qualify. "Reduced" sets all verticals to 25, adding ~13 eligible recruiters. Changes both schemes.
z factor — sourcing bonus as a fraction of the placer's base (x). Higher z = stronger sharing incentive but higher cost.

Calculation Sources

Recruiter cost (status quo): Each recruiter's current payout uses marginal slab rates — like income tax brackets. SF and non-SF placements are counted separately, each climbing its own slab ladder. Enterprise brackets are 1–35 / 36–70 / 71–150 PRs; Corporate brackets are 1–25 / 26–50 / 51–150 PRs. Rates range from ₹130–300 per PR depending on SF status and slab. Recruiters below their vertical's target earn ₹0.

Recruiter cost (new scheme): Every eligible recruiter earns a flat-per-placement bonus (x) plus a retention bonus (y × months the worker stays, capped at 12). No SF/non-SF distinction. In "Flat" schemes, x is the same for everyone; in "Slab" schemes, x increases with the recruiter's slab tier. The attrition slider increases tenure, which increases both the y×T cost and the markup revenue.

TL & Manager cost (status quo): TLs and Managers earn per-PR rates based on their team's average productivity (total team PRs ÷ headcount). Eligibility requires average productivity to meet the vertical's TL/Manager target. Rates are ₹150/200/250 for TLs and ₹200/250/300 for Managers, with a cap of 100 attributed PRs per person. Current totals: ₹55,100 (TLs) + ₹85,950 (Managers).

TL & Manager cost (new scheme): Same x + y×T structure. TL base rates (x) match recruiter rates; Manager x = recruiter x + ₹50 (reflecting the current ₹50 premium). Slab assignment uses average team productivity. The y component uses each team's average tenure (improved by the attrition slider). PR cap of 100 still applies.

Revenue per recruiter: Each recruiter's revenue = (average monthly markup per placement × tenure in months × number of placements) + total sourcing fees. Markup and sourcing fees are client-specific, computed from each recruiter's actual client mix. The attrition slider increases tenure, which increases markup revenue proportionally.

Cross-sourcing: Cross-sourced placements carry a z cost (= z-factor × placer's x) on every cross-sourced PR. "Net new" placements additionally generate revenue (markup × tenure + sourcing fee per PR) and incur placer cost (x + y×T). "Substituted" placements only add the z cost — they don't change total PR count or revenue.

Δ Margin = (New scheme revenue − New scheme total cost) − (Status quo revenue − Status quo total cost). A positive delta means the new scheme produces higher net margins.
